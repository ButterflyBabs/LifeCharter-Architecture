A founder-led operating system for service businesses, with an executive coach built into every account. This brief supports a one-time ask of $11,500 and is written for investors who want the whole picture, including what is not yet proven.

Executive summary

What it is. LifeCharter Command Suite (LCCS) is a business operating system for the owners of any kind of business. Its first audience is coaches, consultants and professional-service owners. One subscription gives the founder the system (strategy, CRM, sales, marketing, finance, projects, content, compliance and an AI assistant) and the human guidance to run it. Executive coaching leads every plan.

The idea. Software alone does not change how a founder works, and coaching alone leaves no system behind. LCCS pairs them. The coach onboards, audits, plans and holds each client accountable. The software records, scores and automates what the coaching decides, and shows both sides whether the business is getting healthier.

Where we are. The platform is built and live: more than 1,300 commits, over 100 working pages, a CRM with booking and email engines, finance, a scoring model, an AI assistant that acts behind approval, a community app and a certification portal. We are pre-scale. A first cohort of 3 paying Starter clients and 5 Pre-Founder beta testers is onboarding and paid acquisition began in October 2026. The business holds no debt, and monthly income covers existing payroll and monthly expenses. We do not claim a growth curve yet, and this brief says so wherever it matters.

The ask. $11,500, one time. The primary ask is $6,500 for marketing; the secondary ask is $5,000 for the final software builds that complete the Command Suite and its approval on the Apple, Meta and Google platforms. The business is built and already has clients, so the ask accelerates it. The base case reaches 105 paying clients and about $52,000 in monthly recurring revenue (roughly $626,000 annualized) by month 12, at founder prices, funded by client revenue and the founder's own audience. The start date is November 1, 2026, with a 60-day delay before debt service begins on January 15, 2027.

Why it is a good bet

  • Front-loaded contract value. Each client pays a $2,497 to $4,997 implementation fee, then $347 to $997 a month. Average first-year value is about $9,000.

  • Built, not promised. The product risk has largely been paid down. Most of the money goes to reaching people, and the secondary $5,000 to further development.

  • A funnel that already measures itself. Every session reports clicks, registrations, attendance, bookings, closes and cost per close.

  • Leverage in the delivery. Group coaching and software serve many clients per hour of coach time.

  • Owned distribution. A founder-led brand, a recurring live event engine and a 10% affiliate program.

The problem

Founder-led service businesses run on the owner's head. Strategy lives in a notebook, leads in a spreadsheet or three apps, money in an accounting tool nobody reviews, and the owner is the only system. Three failures repeat:

  1. Tool sprawl without a plan. Founders buy a CRM, a scheduler, a course platform and an AI tool, then use a fraction of each. Nothing connects, and no one tells them what to do first.

  2. Advice without implementation. Courses, books and coaching produce insight. None of it is wired into daily work, so it fades within weeks.

  3. No feedback loop. They cannot see whether the business is getting healthier, so they cannot tell whether what they are doing works.

The cost is time (admin crowds out selling), cash (follow-up that leaks, spend nobody reviews) and risk (compliance and succession gaps). For coaches and consultants it also caps growth: the business they sell help for is often the least systematized one in the room.

Why leading with coaching works commercially

  1. Conversion. The buyer is a founder who wants to be told what to do, not handed a login. A consultation converts the problem into a plan, and the plan into the subscription.

  2. Retention. A weekly cadence gives a reason to open the Suite every week. Clients who stop using software churn; clients on a call do not drift.

  3. Differentiation. Pure software competitors cannot copy a trusted human method. Pure coaches cannot copy a working platform.

  4. Compounding data. Every call sees the client's real score and task list, so the coach coaches to evidence, and the product improves from what coaches see.

How the software carries the coach

  • The audit, plans and health score give every call a shared agenda.

  • Tasks, projects and SOPs turn what was decided on Monday into work done by Friday.

  • The AI assistant reads the client's own assessments and acts between calls, always behind an Approve button, so the client stays in control.

  • A coach and client see the same numbers. That is the accountability.

How coaching scales without the founder

Group formats serve every client in one hour of coach time. The plan builds a bench of certified coaches through the Coaching Certification portal (already built, with 423 courses), so that delivery capacity grows with client revenue, not with the founder's calendar.

Why timing matters

Time is of the essence. Year end is when business owners take stock of the current year and decide what the next one will look like, and they spend and commit accordingly. A founder who is guided through that review in November and December enters January with a plan and momentum. One who waits enters it with a resolution.

What the research indicates

  • Q4 is the strongest quarter for software. FastSpring's data from more than 3,500 software companies puts Q4 at 106% of an average quarter's sales, against 97% to 99% for the other three. It attributes part of the B2B lift to buyers using budget before it resets and setting up tools before the new year.

  • Small businesses move at year end. Ramp's year-end spend data shows software spending among small and large SMBs rising 61% and 76%, against about 4% for mid-market and 2% for enterprise.

  • Momentum fades quickly. Psychologist John Norcross of the University of Scranton followed people who made resolutions: 29% had dropped them by mid-January, 36% by February, and more than half by June.

These are third-party datasets, and most are not specific to coaching or to the Command Suite. They are indicators of seasonality, not forecasts.

What this means for the ask

  • Funding on November 1, 2026 reaches the MasterClasses of November and December, the weeks when owners are reviewing the year, before the January planning season.

  • Founder pricing ends on December 31, 2026. Clients who join before then keep it for the life of their contract, which gives the year-end audience a reason to act now.

  • A delay is not neutral. Each month later pushes the first measured cost per registrant, the first retention data and the January pricing decision further out.
